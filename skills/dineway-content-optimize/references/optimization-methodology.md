# Content Optimization Method

Use this method to turn current evidence into selective edits. The active native quality policy,
not a universal score, decides release eligibility.

## Baseline

Compare the exact local Draft against its approved Brief, current schema, source pack, selected
SERP competitors, published site inventory, byline samples, and native quality policy. Keep the
same evidence set within a pass so before/after results remain comparable.

Classify findings by the concrete weakness they address:

- coverage: a supported subtopic or user question is missing;
- depth: a present section lacks evidence, specificity, or practical detail;
- structure: headings, direct answers, lists, tables, or section order obstruct extraction;
- metadata: title, description, canonical, robots, or structured fields are incomplete;
- evidence: a material claim lacks a usable source or clear attribution;
- voice/readability: copy is repetitive, generic, unnatural, or mismatched to the byline;
- links/media/accessibility: a required target, asset, caption, dimension, or alt text is invalid.

## Prioritize and iterate

1. Mark schema, identity, policy, provenance, required media, accessibility, and critical integrity
   failures as blocking.
2. Mark changes with direct intent, evidence, or usability impact as high priority.
3. Keep contextual depth, heading, and word-count suggestions medium unless evidence makes them
   essential. Length is never a goal by itself.
4. Treat minor wording as low priority and explicitly reject changes that cause keyword stuffing,
   unsupported expansion, competitor imitation, or voice loss.
5. Apply one coherent group of edits, then re-evaluate the entire Draft against the unchanged
   baseline. Stop after three broad passes and record unresolved tradeoffs.

Interpret every score relative to its active threshold and supporting checks. A high aggregate
score cannot override a failed mandatory gate; a below-threshold dimension identifies where to
work, not permission to fabricate material. For high-value or highly competitive content, propose
stricter versioned thresholds through the native policy path rather than embedding special numbers
in the Draft or Skill.

## Citability patterns

Use these only when they fit the audience and evidence:

- answer the primary question in the first useful paragraph;
- express one clear claim, attach a named source, then state the practical implication;
- use concise definitions for unfamiliar entities and terms;
- add question-and-answer sections only for real observed questions;
- use comparison tables only when attributes are comparable and source-backed;
- give summaries, lists, or tables as additional extraction surfaces without duplicating prose;
- expose author or byline expertise only when native profile evidence supports it.

Favor quotability, entity clarity, corroborated facts, and structure over keyword density or raw
length. Never assume an AI engine's preferences from a generic platform stereotype.

## Library-level learning

When the same finding recurs across pages, record the affected content IDs and evidence. Propose an
explicit Site Context or versioned policy change when appropriate; do not silently turn a recurring
suggestion into a hidden rule. Verify later Monitor observations before claiming that an edit
improved ranking or citation visibility.
